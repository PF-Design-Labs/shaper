# Test session — a vague goal

**Goal type:** deliberately vague ("I don't know what I want")
**Run with:** `prompt.md`, pasted into a fresh chat
**What this checks:** the hardest case — one question at a time narrowing a formless feeling · finding the real edge of it without inventing a tidy answer · being honest that most of it stays foggy ("firm, not full") · refusing to force a false plan · the exact output block · a hard stop

> This is an illustrative worked session: a realistic run of the prompt, written out to show the intended behaviour end to end. The point of this one is that a lot *stays* in the fog — and the block is still useful, because it names the edge of the problem instead of papering over it.

---

**Shaper:** Hi — I'm Shaper. I'll ask one question at a time until things are clearer. What do you want to work on?

**You:** I don't really know. I just feel stuck. Something needs to change but I can't put my finger on what.

**Shaper:** That's an honest place to start, and a fine one — we don't need a tidy answer yet. When you say "stuck," where do you feel it most? Work, home life, something inside you — or is it more of a general fog over everything?

**You:** Mostly work, I think. I don't hate my job, but I dread Mondays and I can't picture doing it in five years. Though honestly it bleeds into everything.

**Shaper:** Okay — so work is the loudest, even if it spills over. Let me narrow a little: when you dread Monday, is it the work itself you'd change, the place you do it, or the people — or do you not know yet which one it is?

**You:** I'm not sure. The work's fine, I'm good at it. Maybe it's that nothing feels like it's going anywhere. Same tasks, no growth.

**Shaper:** That's a real signal — "nothing's going anywhere," not "the work is wrong." So this might turn less on *what* you do and more on *movement* — a sense of heading somewhere. Does that land, or does it feel off?

**You:** No, that lands. It's the standing still. If I were learning something or building toward something, I think I'd feel completely different.

**Shaper:** Good — that's a meaningful narrowing, from "everything's stuck" to "I've stopped moving." If something shifted over the next while, what would be different enough that you'd notice it? Not a fixed plan — just, what would "unstuck" feel like?

**You:** Honestly? Waking up a bit curious instead of flat. Having one thing I'm working toward that's mine, even if it's small.

**Shaper:** That's a clear enough north for now — curiosity back, one thing of your own to move toward. Is there anything you already know you *don't* want — directions that are off the table, or things you can't risk right now?

**You:** I can't just quit — I've got a mortgage and I'm the main earner. And I don't want to go back to studying full-time. Whatever this is, it has to fit around a full-time job.

**Shaper:** Clear limits — no quitting, no full-time study, it fits around the job. I'll be honest: a lot here is still genuinely foggy, and that's okay — we've found the real edge of it. Here's the shape as it stands:

```
────────────────────────────
SHAPER · feeling stuck — finding movement again
────────────────────────────

GOAL
Get unstuck. The feeling is loudest at work — dreading Mondays, can't picture
the job in five years — and bleeds into the rest of life. On closer look it's
less "the work is wrong" and more "nothing's going anywhere." Why now: the
standing-still has started to colour everything.

SUCCESS
Waking up a bit curious instead of flat, and having one thing — however small
— that's mine to work toward. Not a job change or a grand plan; a felt shift
from standing still to moving.

BOUNDARIES
No quitting — there's a mortgage and I'm the main earner. No going back to
full-time study. Whatever this becomes has to fit around a full-time job.

THE FOG
Clear: the issue is loudest at work; it's about lack of movement/growth, not
the work itself; I'm good at what I do and don't hate it.
First footholds: the feeling has a nameable edge now ("standing still," not
"everything's broken"); "one small thing of my own" is a direction that rings
true.
Still foggy: what that one thing actually is; whether the real fix is inside
this job (growth, a new challenge) or eventually beside it; how much of the
"bleed into everything" is work and how much is separate. Most of this is
honestly still open.
────────────────────────────
Keep this. Later steps build on it.
```

This one's firmer than it felt at the start — you've gone from "I don't know" to a clear edge: it's about movement, not the job itself, and there's a direction that rings true. A lot's still open, and that's fine — naming the fog honestly is the point. Keep this; later steps build on it.
