# Test session — a business goal

**Goal type:** small business (mkb) growth
**Run with:** `prompt.md`, pasted into a fresh chat
**What this checks:** one question at a time · clarifying before suggesting · naming the one thing it turns on (capacity) · keeping an unknown honest instead of guessing past it · stopping at "firm, not full" · the exact output block · a hard stop with no plan

> This is an illustrative worked session: a realistic run of the prompt, written out to show the intended behaviour end to end. Your own wording will differ from tool to tool, but the shape — one question per turn, then the block, then stop — is what to expect.

---

**Shaper:** Hi — I'm Shaper. I'll ask you questions one at a time until your goal is clear. What do you want to work on?

**You:** I run a small bakery. I want to start supplying bread to cafés in town.

**Shaper:** Nice — a real growth step. Before we get into how, I want to understand the pull: why cafés, and why now? What's making this feel like the right move at this point?

**You:** Walk-in sales are flat, and I've got oven capacity in the mornings sitting unused. A café owner I know asked if I could supply her, and that got me thinking there might be a few of them.

**Shaper:** That unused morning capacity is a strong reason — you'd be turning something you already pay for into income. When you picture this working well, say a year from now, what does it actually look like? How many cafés, how much bread — or is it more about the money it brings in?

**You:** I'd be happy with four or five regular café accounts, maybe 150–200 loaves a week on top of what I do now. Mainly it's about steadier income I can count on, versus walk-in which swings a lot.

**Shaper:** So "steady, predictable orders" is the real prize, more than the headline number. Here's the thing I think this turns on — tell me if I'm wrong: supplying cafés means baking to someone else's schedule and volume, reliably, every week — a different rhythm than a shop counter. Do you already know your mornings can take that on without dropping quality or burning you out? Or is that still an open question?

**You:** Honestly, that's the part I'm unsure about. One or two cafés, easy. Five might mean hiring or a second oven, and I haven't worked that out.

**Shaper:** Good — worth naming clearly rather than guessing past. So the capacity ceiling is a real unknown we'll carry forward. Let me ask about limits from the other side: is there anything you're not willing to do to make this work? Dropping your walk-in trade, taking on debt for equipment, baking seven days a week?

**You:** I won't give up the shopfront — that's the heart of it. And I don't want a big loan. A part-time hire I could stomach; a second oven only if the numbers were obvious.

**Shaper:** Clear boundaries — shopfront stays, no big debt, equipment only if it plainly pays for itself. One more, on success: how would you know, in the first few months, that this is working and not quietly costing you? Is there a signal you'd watch?

**You:** If I had two or three cafés reordering every week without me chasing them, and it wasn't eating into my shop baking or my evenings, I'd call it working.

**Shaper:** That's a clean signal — repeat orders without chasing, and no bleed into the shop or your time. I think we've got enough to be useful. Here's the shape of it:

```
────────────────────────────
SHAPER · supplying bread to local cafés
────────────────────────────

GOAL
Turn unused morning oven capacity into steady wholesale income by supplying
bread to cafés in town. The pull is predictable, countable orders — walk-in
sales are flat and swing a lot — and one café owner has already asked.

SUCCESS
Two or three cafés reordering every week without being chased, within the
first few months, without eating into shop baking or evenings. Longer term:
four or five regular accounts, roughly 150–200 loaves a week on top of
current trade.

BOUNDARIES
The shopfront stays — it's the heart of the business and won't be dropped.
No big loan. A part-time hire is acceptable; a second oven only if the
numbers plainly justify it.

THE FOG
Clear: there's real unused morning capacity; at least one café is interested;
the goal is steadiness, not a big headline number.
First footholds: the café who already asked is a natural first account and a
low-risk test; current mornings comfortably cover one or two cafés.
Still foggy: whether mornings can handle four or five accounts without a hire
or a second oven — the capacity ceiling is unworked-out; café pricing and
margins aren't known yet.
────────────────────────────
Keep this. Later steps build on it.
```

That's the shape, and it's firm enough to build on. The capacity question is the one thing left genuinely open — worth keeping in view. Hang on to this; later steps build on exactly this.
