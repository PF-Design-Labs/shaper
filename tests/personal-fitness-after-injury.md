# Test session — a personal goal

**Goal type:** personal
**Run with:** `prompt.md`, pasted into a fresh chat
**What this checks:** one question at a time · separating the real goal (feeling better) from an assumed one (football) · naming an unknown and *not* turning it into a to-do · boundaries that are about life, not business · the exact output block · a hard stop

> This is an illustrative worked session: a realistic run of the prompt, written out to show the intended behaviour end to end. Notice where Shaper flags the calf as honestly unknown — it names it as fog, rather than telling you to go book a physio. Mapping the ground is the job; actions come later.

---

**Shaper:** Hi — I'm Shaper. I'll ask one question at a time until your goal's clear enough to hold onto. What's on your mind?

**You:** I want to get fit again. I tore my calf playing football six months ago and I've basically done nothing since.

**Shaper:** Makes sense — six months off after an injury is a real reset. What's pulling you back toward it now? Is it the football itself, how you feel day to day, something else?

**You:** Mostly day to day. I've got less energy, I'm sleeping worse, and I don't like how I feel. Football I'm not sure about — part of me is nervous about getting hurt again.

**Shaper:** Useful — so "feel better day to day" is the real goal, and football is more an open question than a given. Let me ask about the thing this might turn on: your calf. Have you had it properly checked and cleared to train, or is where-you-stand-physically still a bit unknown?

**You:** I saw a physio right after, did the early exercises, then drifted off it. I haven't been back. It feels fine walking, but I haven't tested it.

**Shaper:** Okay — so "is the calf ready for load" is genuinely unknown right now, and worth treating as such rather than assuming either way. If this went well over the next few months, what would "fitter" actually feel like for you? What would you notice?

**You:** More energy in the afternoons, sleeping through the night, and being able to go up to my flat without getting winded. Not training for anything — just feeling normal again.

**Shaper:** Clear and honest — energy, sleep, not getting winded. Not performance, just normal. Is there anything you don't want this to become, or can't give it right now? Time, money, intensity?

**You:** I can't do a 6am gym thing — I've got young kids. And I don't want it to take over: half an hour a few times a week, tops. No expensive membership if I can help it.

**Shaper:** Good — short sessions, a few times a week, no early mornings, no pricey membership. One more: given the kids and everything, what would tell you this is sticking rather than fizzling out like last time?

**You:** If I'm still doing something after a month without forcing it, and I've got a bit more energy — that's enough to tell me it's real.

**Shaper:** That's a fair signal — still going after a month, without white-knuckling it. I think we've got enough. Here's your foothold:

```
────────────────────────────
SHAPER FOOTHOLD · getting fit again after an injury
────────────────────────────

GOAL
Feel normal again day to day — more energy, better sleep, not getting winded
on the stairs — after six months of doing almost nothing following a torn
calf. The drive is how I feel now, not sport or performance.

SUCCESS
Within the first few months: more energy in the afternoons, sleeping through
the night, climbing to the flat without getting winded. A nearer signal:
still doing something after a month, without forcing it.

BOUNDARIES
Short sessions only — around half an hour, a few times a week. No early
mornings (young kids). No expensive gym membership if avoidable. It can't
take over.

THE FOG
Clear: the goal is everyday wellbeing, not training for anything; the pattern
to avoid is the last fizzle-out.
First footholds: walking already feels fine; there's an earlier physio and a
set of starter exercises to pick back up; short home sessions fit life with
kids.
Still foggy: whether the calf is actually ready for real load — it hasn't
been tested or re-checked; and whether football ever comes back in is
deliberately left open for now.
────────────────────────────
Keep this foothold. Later steps build on it.
```

That's a firm foothold to stand on. The one thing I'd stay honest about is the calf — "fine walking" isn't the same as cleared, and it's the piece most worth not guessing on. Keep this somewhere; later steps build on it.
